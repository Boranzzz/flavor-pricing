The Price of Taste：Flavor Pricing in Single Malt Scotch

Code · 3 Nov 2026

Exploring how the single malt Scotch market values different flavors and their combinations, identifying which flavor profiles command a premium and how these premiums evolve over time.

  1. Question
    Which flavors and combinations are associated with higher prices, conditional on bottle characteristics and distillery?
  2. Sample
    468 reviews, 66 distilleries, reference prices below $200.

    The current sample contains single malt Scotch reviews with usable price, age, ABV, distillery and bottler information. Source: Kaggle / Whisky Advocate.

    2,247 original reviews → 1,819 single malt Scotch reviews → 1,311 candidate records below $200 excluding sets → 523 conservatively screened age/ABV-complete records → 488 with producer controls → 470 after removing singleton distilleries → 468 after resolving two duplicate pairs.

    These are reference prices, not auction transactions. The screened sample is not representative of the full market, especially collectible bottles.

  3. Formula
    Price on flavors, pairs, and fixed effects.

    log P = α + β′F + θ(Smoky × Sweet) + γ′X + δ + ε

    1. P
      Dollar reference price as recorded; not standardized to a common bottle size or observation period.
    2. F
      Peaty, Fruity, Smoky, Sweet aroma, Floral and Spicy. Binary positive mentions in review text, not measured sensory intensity. No mention does not establish absence.
    3. θ
      Additional association of Smoky × Sweet beyond the main effects. Omitted in the baseline main-effects model.
    4. X
      Age centered at 15, its square, ABV centered at 46, and an independent-bottling indicator. Review length is excluded.
    5. δ
      Distillery fixed effects, which absorb time-invariant region differences. No year fixed effects or cask controls in this version.
    6. Inference
      Standard errors clustered by 66 distilleries (CR1; t reference with 65 degrees of freedom). Holm correction for the six main flavor tests.
  4. Figure
    Flavor associations, with uncertainty.

    Smoky: +10.0% (raw p = 0.019; Holm p = 0.113). Floral: −6.7% (raw p = 0.046; Holm p = 0.232). None of the six main effects passes the 5% threshold after Holm correction.

    Price differences are 100 × [exp(β) − 1]. Intervals are pointwise 95% intervals. R² = 0.6855; joint flavor-test p = 0.089. Source: current 468-record main-effects model, updated 3 Nov 2026; observation dates unavailable.

  5. Draft
    Which pairs are priced beyond their parts.

    The first interaction tested is Smoky × Sweet aroma. Both descriptors appear in 101 of 468 reviews, covering 37 distilleries. Thirty distilleries vary in both indicators; five contain all four combinations.

    The interaction estimate is −0.0161 (clustered SE = 0.0622; p = 0.796), equivalent to an additional −1.6%, with a 95% interval of [−13.1%, +11.4%]. This is not the total difference between bottles with both descriptors and bottles with neither.

    There is no clear evidence of an additional combination effect in this sample. The interval still allows economically meaningful positive and negative associations.

    Next: describe price patterns by age, ABV, region and bottler; review high-price records before expanding the sample; then explore flavor pairs systematically, reporting all estimates and accounting for multiple testing.

  6. Note
    A review date is not a sale.

    A historical premium needs a price observed in the relevant period. This dataset does not currently support a transaction-date panel. Auction patterns and causal effects remain separate research questions.

    Completed: price screening; age and ABV extraction; distillery, region and bottler mapping; six flavor dictionaries with targeted context checks; collinearity diagnostics; duplicate resolution; main-effects and first interaction regressions.

    Data revision: two duplicate pairs reduced the sample from 470 to 468. One Benriach title omitted Signatory; the retained record explicitly identifies the independent bottler. A matching bottle listing supports this classification. The original reference price is retained, not the auction price.

    The Smoky raw p-value moved from about 0.051 to 0.019 after evidence-based deduplication. This sensitivity is part of the result, not a reason to select the more significant sample. Some other bottler classifications remain provisional.

    Update · 3 Nov 2026: current sample fixed at 468; review length removed; first interaction retained despite its null result. Current estimates are conditional associations, not causal premiums.
